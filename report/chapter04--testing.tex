\section{Faults identified}
The final run produced 12 failures across 62 tests. These failures
were grouped into the eight faults in Table~\ref{tab:faults}. Some
faults were exposed by more than one test.

\subsection{How the tests exposed the faults}
\paragraph{Regular quantity threshold.}
The four-unit and six-unit cases passed, but the five-unit case returned
\pounds0.00 instead of \pounds5.00. The difference at the boundary
pointed to the rate selection in \texttt{DiscountService}, where
line 133 uses \texttt{quantity > 5} and excludes exactly five.

\paragraph{Same-name non-accessory entries.}
Three laptops in a single entry qualified for the VIP discount, but
splitting them into same-name entries of one and two gave no discount.
The quantity loop in \texttt{DiscountService} passes each entry's
quantity to the rate calculation at lines 156--159, so neither entry
reaches the threshold on its own.

\paragraph{Same-name accessory entries.}
The test split three same-name mice into entries of one and two.
It expected \pounds3.00 off and got \pounds0.00. The accessory loop
in \texttt{DiscountService} skips an entry if its own quantity is below
three (lines 178--183), so both entries were skipped before any
discount was calculated.

\paragraph{VIP15 eligibility subtotal.}
The test verified that the original subtotal was \pounds150.00 and the
quantity discount was \pounds15.00 before the coupon assertion failed.
Only the original amount qualified. The eligibility check at lines
223--225 of \texttt{DiscountService} compares the reduced
\pounds135.00 product total with the \pounds150.00 minimum.

\paragraph{Case-sensitive coupon matching.}
The eligible lower-case \texttt{discount10} input with surrounding
spaces raised an exception. The stack trace pointed to line 235 of
\texttt{DiscountService}. Reading back from that exception showed that
line 206 trims the spaces, while line 212 compares the remaining text
with the upper-case code using a case-sensitive comparison.

\paragraph{Ineligible coupons were ignored.}
Four tests expected a rejection for a recognised coupon when the total
or customer type did not qualify. All returned normally. Following
the failed eligibility checks in \texttt{DiscountService} led to
lines 219 and 231, which return a zero discount instead of throwing
the expected exception.

\paragraph{VIP Express delivery fee.}
A VIP Express order was charged \pounds0.00, while the Regular and
Premium Express tests returned the expected \pounds9.99. This pointed
to the VIP condition in \texttt{DeliveryService}. The return at
lines 30--31 occurs before the Express check at line 34.

\paragraph{Subtotal passed to delivery.}
The integration test confirmed the \pounds74.10 discounted total but
observed free delivery. The Mockito test then showed that the delivery
call used \pounds100.00 where \pounds90.00 was expected. The
invocation trace pointed to \texttt{OrderService}, where line 53
passes the original subtotal.

\subsection{Coverage gains and remaining gaps}
The baseline left gaps in cart and delivery validation. Tests for
invalid quantities, insufficient stock, and missing cart entries raised
\texttt{ShoppingCart} branch coverage from 18 of 24 to 24 of 24. These
paths were prioritised because invalid updates can affect cart contents
and downstream totals. The negative-total test raised
\texttt{DeliveryService} branch coverage from 10 of 12 to 11 of 12 by
checking that an invalid merchandise total was rejected before a fee
was calculated.

The remaining VIP branch in the Standard delivery helper could not be
reached because the public method returns earlier for VIPs. Additional
tests for customer and product setters were lower priority because they
would add less evidence about the pricing rules in the brief.
